## Result for RQ1
With year fixed effects and neighborhood-clustered standard errors, the unadjusted PM2.5 coefficient was 10.291 for the child asthma ED rate (95% CI: -17.942 to 38.525; p=0.475; 378 rows) and -2.433 for the adult age-adjusted rate (95% CI: -16.947 to 12.081; p=0.743; 336 rows). Both 95% confidence intervals include zero. Coefficients are per 1 mcg/m3 increase in annual mean PM2.5.

## Result for RQ2
After controlling for annual poverty, the PM2.5 coefficient was 8.073 for the child rate (95% CI: -6.942 to 23.087; p=0.292; 378 rows) and -2.836 for the adult age-adjusted rate (95% CI: -10.389 to 4.717; p=0.462; 336 rows). Both 95% confidence intervals include zero.

## Robustness
Using the consistent 2019-23 poverty estimate (Robustness A), coefficients were 10.641 for children (95% CI: -3.249 to 24.531; p=0.133) and -1.323 for adults (95% CI: -8.140 to 5.493; p=0.704). Excluding the three flagged child rows (Robustness B) gave a child coefficient of 8.012 (95% CI: -7.362 to 23.387; p=0.307; 375 rows). Using the raw adult rate (Robustness C) gave a coefficient of -3.262 (95% CI: -10.896 to 4.373; p=0.402; 336 rows). Excluding 2020 and 2021 (Robustness D) gave coefficients of 8.545 for children (95% CI: -7.747 to 24.838; p=0.304; 294 rows) and -2.742 for adults (95% CI: -10.469 to 4.984; p=0.487; 252 rows). Each 95% confidence interval includes zero.

Within-year Pearson correlations between PM2.5 and poverty ranged from -0.075 in 2020 to 0.118 in 2015 (42 neighborhoods per year).

## Limitations
- Correlation is not causation.
- Neighborhood-level data cannot describe individuals (ecological fallacy).
- Poverty is measured in overlapping 5-year windows, so it is a rough control.
- Outcome is observed ED visits only, not asthma prevalence.
